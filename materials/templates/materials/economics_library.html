Basic Economic Concepts
Opportunity Cost & Trade-offs Intro
Opportunity Cost = Value of Next Best Alternative
Economic Profit = Total Revenue - (Explicit Costs + Implicit Costs)
Accounting Profit = Total Revenue - Explicit Costs

Explicit costs are actual monetary payments. Implicit costs are opportunity costs of using owned resources.

Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF) Intro
PPF: Maximum output combinations of two goods given resources & technology
Shape: Concave (bowed out) = Increasing opportunity cost
Straight line = Constant opportunity cost
Convex (bowed in) = Decreasing opportunity cost (rare)
Key Points:
  • Points on the curve: Efficient production
  • Points inside the curve: Inefficient production (unemployment)
  • Points outside the curve: Unattainable with current resources
  • Outward shift: Economic growth (more resources/better tech)
  • Inward shift: Economic contraction (resource loss/tech decline)
Marginal Analysis Intro
Marginal Benefit (MB) = Additional benefit from one more unit
Marginal Cost (MC) = Additional cost from one more unit
Optimal Quantity: MB = MC
Net Benefit = Total Benefit - Total Cost

Rational decision makers continue an activity as long as MB ≥ MC.

Supply & Demand
Demand Law & Curve Intro
Law of Demand: As price ↑, quantity demanded ↓ (ceteris paribus)
Demand Function: Qd = a - bP (where a, b > 0)
Inverse Demand: P = (a/b) - (1/b)Qd
Determinants of Demand (shift factors):
  • Income (normal vs inferior goods)
  • Prices of related goods (substitutes/complements)
  • Tastes & preferences
  • Expectations about future prices
  • Number of buyers
Supply Law & Curve Intro
Law of Supply: As price ↑, quantity supplied ↑ (ceteris paribus)
Supply Function: Qs = c + dP (where c, d ≥ 0)
Inverse Supply: P = (c/d) + (1/d)Qs
Determinants of Supply (shift factors):
  • Input prices
  • Technology
  • Prices of related goods (substitutes in production)
  • Expectations about future prices
  • Number of sellers
  • Taxes & subsidies
Market Equilibrium Intro
Equilibrium Condition: Qd = Qs
Solve: a - bP = c + dP → P* = (a - c)/(b + d)
Then: Q* = a - bP* = c + dP*
Consumer Surplus (CS) = Area below demand curve & above price
Producer Surplus (PS) = Area above supply curve & below price
Total Surplus = CS + PS (maximized at equilibrium)
Deadweight Loss (DWL) = Loss of total surplus from market inefficiency

At equilibrium: No shortage or surplus. Markets tend toward equilibrium through price adjustments.

Price Controls & Quotas Intermediate
Price Ceiling (max legal price):
• Binding if Pceiling < P* → Shortage = Qd - Qs at Pceiling
• Non-binding if Pceiling ≥ P* → No effect

Price Floor (min legal price):
• Binding if Pfloor > P* → Surplus = Qs - Qd at Pfloor
• Non-binding if Pfloor ≤ P* → No effect
Quota (limit on quantity):
• Creates shortage if Qquota < Q*
• Leads to black market & rent-seeking behavior
Elasticity
Price Elasticity of Demand Intro
Ed = (%ΔQd) / (%ΔP) = (ΔQd/Qd) / (ΔP/P)
Midpoint Formula: Ed = [(Q2-Q1)/((Q2+Q1)/2)] / [(P2-P1)/((P2+P1)/2)]
Interpretation:
  • |Ed| > 1: Elastic (sensitive to price)
  • |Ed| = 1: Unit elastic
  • |Ed| < 1: Inelastic (insensitive to price)
  • Ed = 0: Perfectly inelastic (vertical demand)
  • |Ed| → ∞: Perfectly elastic (horizontal demand)

Total Revenue Test: If demand is elastic, price ↑ → TR ↓. If inelastic, price ↑ → TR ↑.

Income & Cross-Price Elasticity Intermediate
Income Elasticity: EI = (%ΔQd) / (%ΔI)
• EI > 0: Normal good
• EI > 1: Luxury good
• 0 < Ei < 1: Necessity
• EI < 0: Inferior good

Cross-Price Elasticity: Exy = (%ΔQd,x) / (%ΔPy)
• Exy > 0: Substitutes
• Exy < 0: Complements
• Exy = 0: Unrelated goods
Consumer Theory
Utility Maximization Intermediate
Maximize: U(x, y) subject to: Pxx + Pyy = I
Tangency Condition: MRSxy = Px/Py
Where MRSxy = MUx/MUy (Marginal Rate of Substitution)
And MUx = ∂U/∂x, MUy = ∂U/∂y
Solution Methods:
  • Graphical: Indifference curve tangent to budget line
  • Analytical: Lagrange multiplier or substitution
  • Corner solutions possible for perfect substitutes/complements
Demand from Utility Maximization Advanced
For Cobb-Douglas: U = xαyβ
Demand: x* = αI/Px, y* = βI/Py
(where α + β = 1)

For Perfect Substitutes: U = ax + by
Choose good with higher utility per dollar: max(a/Px, b/Py)

For Perfect Complements: U = min(ax, by)
Demand: x* = y* = I/(aPx + bPy)
Production & Costs
Production Function Intro
Q = f(L, K) where Q = output, L = labor, K = capital
Marginal Product of Labor: MPL = ΔQ/ΔL = ∂Q/∂L
Average Product of Labor: APL = Q/L
Law of Diminishing MP: As L ↑ (holding K constant), MPL eventually ↓
Cost Functions Intermediate
Total Cost (TC) = Fixed Cost (FC) + Variable Cost (VC)
Average Cost (AC) = TC/Q
Marginal Cost (MC) = ΔTC/ΔQ = dTC/dQ

Relationships:
• MC < AC ⇒ AC falling
• MC = AC ⇒ AC at minimum
• MC > AC ⇒ AC rising
• MC crosses AVC and ATC at their minimum points
Short-Run vs Long-Run Costs Intermediate
Short Run: At least one input fixed (usually K)
Long Run: All inputs variable

Long-Run Average Cost (LRAC) envelope of all SRAC curves
Economies of Scale: LRAC ↓ as Q ↑
Diseconomies of Scale: LRAC ↑ as Q ↑
Constant Returns to Scale: LRAC constant as Q ↑
Returns to Scale (production side):
  • Increasing: f(tL, tK) > t·f(L, K) for t > 1
  • Constant: f(tL, tK) = t·f(L, K)
  • Decreasing: f(tL, tK) < t·f(L, K) for t > 1
Market Structures
Intro
Conditions:
• Many buyers & sellers
• Homogeneous product
• Free entry & exit
• Perfect information

Firm Behavior:
• Price taker: P = MR
• Profit max: MR = MC → P = MC
• Shutdown rule: Produce if P ≥ min AVC
• Break-even: P = min ATC
Market Outcomes:
  • Short run: May have economic profit/loss
  • Long run: Economic profit = 0 (normal profit only)
  • P = min ATC = MC (productive & allocative efficiency)
Intermediate
Demand faced by firm: Market demand curve
Marginal Revenue: MR = P + Q(dP/dQ) < P (for downward-sloping demand)
Profit max: MR = MC
Markup over MC: (P - MC)/P = 1/|Ed| (Lerner Index)

Deadweight Loss: Area between demand & MC from Qm to Qc

Monopolies produce less & charge more than competitive markets → inefficiency.

Advanced
Monopolistic Competition:
• Many firms, differentiated product
• Free entry/exit → zero economic profit in LR
• Excess capacity: Produce where ATC > min ATC

Oligopoly Models:
• Cournot: Firms choose quantity simultaneously
Reaction function: qi = f(qj)
Nash equilibrium: Best responses mutual
• Bertrand: Firms choose price simultaneously
Homogeneous goods: P = MC (Bertrand paradox)
Differentiated goods: P > MC
• Stackelberg: Leader-follower quantity game
Characteristics:
• Few large firms dominate market
• Significant barriers to entry
• Interdependent decision-making
• May collude (cartels) or compete
• Game theory essential for analysis
Macroeconomics
GDP Measurement Intro
Expenditure Approach: GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)
• C = Consumption, I = Investment, G = Government spending
• X = Exports, M = Imports

Income Approach: GDP = Wages + Rent + Interest + Profit
Production Approach: Sum of value added across all industries

Nominal GDP: Current prices
Real GDP: Base-year prices (adjusts for inflation)
GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100
Unemployment & Inflation Intermediate
Unemployment Rate = (Unemployed / Labor Force) × 100
Labor Force = Employed + Unemployed (actively seeking)

Types of Unemployment:
• Frictional: Between jobs/searching
• Structural: Skills/location mismatch
• Cyclical: Due to business downturns
• Seasonal: Regular predictable patterns

Natural Rate = Frictional + Structural
Actual UR = Natural Rate + Cyclical

Inflation Rate = [(CPIt - CPIt-1) / CPIt-1] × 100
CPI = Cost of fixed basket of goods/services today vs base period
Related Concepts:
  • Phillips Curve: Short-run tradeoff between inflation & unemployment
  • Stagflation: High inflation + high unemployment
  • Deflation: Negative inflation rate
  • Hyperinflation: Extremely high (>50% monthly) inflation
Aggregate Demand & Supply Intermediate
AD Curve: Shows relationship between price level & real GDP demanded
AD = C + I + G + (X - M), where components depend on P
Downward slope due to:
• Wealth effect (P ↑ → real wealth ↓ → C ↓)
• Interest rate effect (P ↑ → money demand ↑ → r ↓ → I ↓)
• Exchange rate effect (P ↑ → domestic goods ↓ → NX ↓)

AS Curve (Short Run): Shows relationship between price level & real GDP supplied
Upward slope due to sticky wages/prices
LRAS: Vertical at potential output (YP)
Macroeconomic Equilibrium: Where AD = AS

Recessionary Gap: Y < YP (unemployment)
Inflationary Gap: Y > YP (inflation pressure)
Self-correction: Wage adjustments shift AS over time
Fiscal Policy
Government Budget Intro
Budget Balance = Tax Revenue - Government Spending
• Surplus: Positive (T > G)
• Deficit: Negative (T < G)
• Balance: Zero (T = G)

Primary Balance = Excludes interest payments on debt
Structural Balance: Adjusts for business cycle effects
Debt-to-GDP Ratio: Measures sustainability
Tax Incidence & Multipliers Intermediate
Tax Incidence depends on relative elasticities:
Burden on consumers: Es / (Es - Ed)
Burden on producers: -Ed / (Es - Ed)
(where Ed < 0, Es > 0)

Spending Multiplier: ΔY/ΔG = 1/(1 - MPC)
Tax Multiplier: ΔY/ΔT = -MPC/(1 - MPC)
Balanced Budget Multiplier: ΔY/ΔG = 1 (when ΔT = ΔG)
MPC = Marginal Propensity to Consume (0 < MPC < 1)
MPS = Marginal Propensity to Save = 1 - MPC
Monetary Policy
Money Supply & Demand Intro
Money Supply (Ms): Set by central bank (exogenous)
Money Demand (Md): L = kY - hi (transactions + speculative)
• k = income sensitivity, h = interest sensitivity

Money Market Equilibrium: Ms = Md
Solve for i: i = (kY - Ms) / h

Money Multiplier: m = 1/rr (where rr = required reserve ratio)
Actual multiplier < 1/rr due to cash drain & excess reserves
LM Curve & IS-LM Model Intermediate
LM Curve: Money market equilibrium
Derived from: M/P = L(Y, i) → i = (kY - M/P)/h
Upward slope: Y ↑ → money demand ↑ → i ↑ to restore equilibrium

IS Curve: Goods market equilibrium
Derived from: Y = C(Y - T) + I(i) + G + NX
Downward slope: i ↑ → I ↓ → Y ↓

Equilibrium: Intersection of IS & LM curves
Determines Y* and i*
Monetary Transmission & Phillips Curve Advanced
Monetary Transmission Mechanism:
Money supply ↑ → i ↓ → I ↑ → Y ↑ (via AD shift)

Expectations-Augmented Phillips Curve:
π = πe - β(u - un) + ν
• π = actual inflation, πe = expected inflation
• u = unemployment rate, un = natural rate
• β > 0, ν = supply shock

Long-Run Phillips Curve: Vertical at u = un
Adaptive Expectations: πe = π-1
Rational Expectations: πe = E[π|Ω]
International Trade
Comparative Advantage Intro
Opportunity Cost Approach:
Country A has CA in good X if: (OCA(X) < OCB(X))
Where OC = amount of Y given up to produce one unit of X

Labor Productivity Approach (Ricardian Model):
Country A has CA in X if: (aAX/aAY) < (aBX/aBY)
Where aij = labor needed to produce one unit of good i in country j
Gains from Trade:
  • Both countries can consume beyond their individual PPFs
  • Specialization according to comparative advantage increases world output
  • Terms of trade determine distribution of gains
Tariffs & Quotas Intermediate
Tariff (t): Tax on imports
Effects:
• Domestic price rises: Pd = Pw(1 + t)
• Domestic production ↑, consumption ↓, imports ↓
• Government revenue: t × imports
• Consumer loss: Area under demand curve above Pd
• Producer gain: Area above supply curve below Pd
• Deadweight loss: Production + consumption inefficiency
• Net welfare effect: Usually negative for small country
Quota (Qquota): Limit on import quantity
Equivalent tariff: t such that imports with tariff = Qquota
Effects similar to tariff but:
• No government revenue (unless auctioned)
• Quota rents accrue to license holders
• May lead to corruption & rent-seeking
Econometrics
Simple Linear Regression Intro
Population Model: yi = β0 + β1xi + ui
Sample Regression: ŷi = b0 + b1xi
OLS Estimators:
b1 = Σ(xi - x̄)(yi - ȳ) / Σ(xi - x̄)2
b0 = ȳ - b1x̄

Properties (Gauss-Markov):
• Unbiased: E(b̂) = β
• Minimum variance among linear unbiased estimators
• Consistent & asymptotically normal
Goodness of Fit & Inference Intermediate
Total Sum of Squares: SST = Σ(yi - ȳ)2
Explained Sum of Squares: SSE = Σ(ŷi - ȳ)2 Residual Sum of Squares: SSR = Σ(yi - ŷi)2
SST = SSE + SSR

R2 = SSE/SST = 1 - (SSR/SST)
Adjusted R2 = 1 - [(1-R2)(n-1)/(n-k-1)]
Standard Error of Regression: SER = √(SSR/(n-2))

Sampling Variance: Var(b̂1) = σ2 / Σ(xi - x̄)2
Estimated: s2(b̂1) = s2 / Σ(xi - x̄)2
where s2 = SSR/(n-2)
Hypothesis Testing (two-tailed):
H0: β1 = 0 vs H1: β1 ≠ 0
t-statistic: t = b̂1 / s.e.(b̂1)
Reject H0 if |t| > tcrit(α/2, df=n-2)

Confidence Interval: b̂1 ± tcrit(α/2, df) × s.e.(b̂1)
p-value: P(|t| > |tobs|) under H0
