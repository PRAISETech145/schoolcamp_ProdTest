Mechanics
Momentum & Impulse Basic
Momentum: p = mv [kg·m/s]
Impulse: J = FΔt = Δp [N·s]
Conservation: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂

m = mass, v = velocity, u = initial velocity.

Linear speed: v = ωr
Centripetal accel: a = v²/r = ω²r [m/s²]
Centripetal force: F = mv²/r = mω²r [N]

Kinematics
Equations of Motion (SUVAT) Basic
v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
s = ½(u + v)t
s = vt − ½at²

s = displacement, u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time.

Max height: H = u²sin²θ / (2g)

Maximum range when θ = 45°. g ≈ 9.8 m/s².

Forces & Newton's Laws
Newton's Laws Basic
1st Law: Object remains at rest or uniform motion unless net force acts.
2nd Law: F = ma [N]
3rd Law: Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
Friction Basic
Static friction: f ≤ μₛN
Kinetic friction: f = μₖN
Normal (flat): N = mg
Normal (incline θ): N = mgcosθ

μ = coefficient of friction, N = normal reaction.

Hooke's Law — Springs Basic
F = kx [N]
Elastic PE: E = ½kx² [J]
Series: 1/k = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂
Parallel: k = k₁ + k₂

k = spring constant (N/m), x = extension/compression.

Weight & Apparent Weight Basic
Weight: W = mg [N]
Apparent (lift up): W' = m(g + a)
Apparent (lift down): W' = m(g − a)
Work, Energy & Power
Work & Energy Basic
Work: W = Fdcosθ [J]
Kinetic Energy: KE = ½mv² [J]
Gravitational PE: PE = mgh [J]
Work-Energy Theorem: Wnet = ΔKE
Conservation: KE + PE = constant
Power & Efficiency Basic
Power: P = W/t = Fv [W]
Efficiency: η = (useful output / total input) × 100%
Waves & Oscillations
Wave Properties Basic

Angular frequency: ω = 2πf [rad/s]

Key Physics Experiments
1. Young's Double-Slit Experiment Optics / Waves Intermediate
Aim
Demonstrate the wave nature of light by producing interference fringes.
Setup
Monochromatic light → single slit (coherent source) → double slit (separation d) → screen at distance D.
Observation
Alternating bright (constructive) and dark (destructive) fringes appear on the screen.
Key Equations
Fringe spacing: Δy = λD/d
Bright fringe: d sinθ = nλ (n=0,1,2…)
Dark fringe: d sinθ = (n+½)λ
Conclusion
Light behaves as a wave. Fringe spacing increases with longer λ or larger D; decreases with larger d.
Monochromatic light through two slits produces bright & dark fringes
2. Simple Pendulum — Measuring g Mechanics Basic
Aim
Determine the acceleration due to gravity g using a pendulum of known length.
Setup
Bob on string of length L, clamped at top. Displace <15° and release. Time 20 oscillations → find period T.
Key Equations
Period: T = 2π √(L/g)
Therefore: g = 4π²L/T²
Graph: Plot T² vs L; gradient = 4π²/g
Sources of Error
Air resistance, reaction time, large amplitude (violates small-angle approx.), flexible string.
Conclusion
T² is directly proportional to L. Slope of T²–L graph gives g ≈ 9.8 m/s².
Bob displaced by angle θ; restoring force = mg sinθ
3. Ohm's Law — V–I Characteristics Electricity Basic
Aim
Verify Ohm's Law: current through a conductor is proportional to voltage (at constant temperature).
Setup
Resistor + variable resistor (rheostat) in series with ammeter. Voltmeter in parallel across resistor. Vary voltage using rheostat; record V and I.
Key Equations
Ohm's Law: V = IR
Resistance: R = V/I [Ω]
Graph: V vs I → straight line; gradient = R
Ohmic vs Non-Ohmic
Resistor → straight V–I line. Filament bulb / diode → curved (resistance changes with temperature).
Circuit with ammeter (series) and voltmeter (parallel). V–I graph has slope R.
4. Photoelectric Effect Modern Physics Advanced
Aim
Show that light ejects electrons from a metal surface only when its frequency exceeds a threshold f₀, regardless of intensity — proving light is quantised (photons).
Key Observations
• Below threshold frequency → no electrons, even with bright light.
• Above threshold → electrons emitted instantly.
• Intensity only affects number of electrons, not their max KE.
• Higher frequency → higher max KE of photoelectrons.
Key Equations
Einstein's equation: hf = φ + KEmax
Work function: φ = hf₀
KE max: ½mv² = h(f − f₀)
Stopping voltage: eVₛ = KEmax
Conclusion
Light consists of photons of energy E = hf. Wave theory cannot explain the threshold — this was decisive evidence for quantum theory (Einstein 1905).
Photons eject electrons; energy splits into work function φ and kinetic energy
5. Rutherford Alpha-Scattering Experiment Modern Physics Advanced
Aim
Determine the structure of the atom by firing α-particles at a thin gold foil and detecting their deflections.
Observations
• Most α-particles passed straight through → atom is mostly empty space.
• Some deflected by large angles → small, dense, positive nucleus.
• Very few (1 in 8000) bounced back → nucleus is very small but contains most mass.
Key Result
Nuclear radius: r ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ m (fm)
Atom radius: r ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m
Closest approach: ½mv² = kZe²/r
Conclusion
Atom has a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus surrounded by mostly empty space with orbiting electrons. Disproved Thomson's "plum pudding" model.
Most α-particles pass through; only close hits are deflected by the tiny nucleus
6. Diffraction Grating — Measuring Wavelength Optics Intermediate
Aim
Use a diffraction grating to measure the wavelength of monochromatic (or white) light precisely.
Setup
Laser or lamp → diffraction grating (N lines/mm). Measure angle θ to each order maximum on a screen or with a spectrometer.
Key Equations
Grating equation: d sinθ = nλ
Slit spacing: d = 1/N (N = lines per metre)
Resolving power: RP = nN
Advantage over double slit
Many slits give very sharp, bright maxima → much more accurate wavelength measurement.
Grating produces sharp maxima at angles obeying d sinθ = nλ
7. Specific Heat Capacity by Electrical Heating Thermal Basic
Aim
Measure the specific heat capacity c of a metal or water using an electric immersion heater.
Setup
Known mass m of material in insulated calorimeter. Immersion heater connected to ammeter + voltmeter. Heat for time t, record temperature rise ΔT.
Key Equations
Electrical energy: Q = VIt [J]
Heat absorbed: Q = mcΔT
Therefore: c = VIt / (mΔT) [J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹]
Graph: T vs time → gradient = VI/mc
Sources of Error
Heat loss to surroundings (use insulation + cooling correction), heater not fully embedded, thermometer lag.
Insulated calorimeter with heater; measure V, I, t and temperature rise ΔT
8. Faraday's Electromagnetic Induction Electromagnetism Intermediate
Aim
Demonstrate that a changing magnetic flux through a coil induces an EMF (and hence a current).
Setup
Bar magnet moved in/out of solenoid connected to sensitive galvanometer. Observe deflection as flux changes.
Observations
• Magnet moving in → galvanometer deflects one way.
• Magnet moving out → deflects opposite way.
• Faster motion → larger deflection (higher EMF).
• Stationary magnet → zero deflection.
Key Equations
Faraday's Law: ε = −N dΦ/dt
Flux: Φ = BA cosθ [Wb]
Lenz's Law: Induced current opposes the change (− sign)
Moving magnet changes flux Φ through solenoid → galvanometer deflects
9. Millikan Oil Drop Experiment Modern Physics Advanced
Aim
Measure the charge on a single electron e by balancing gravitational and electric forces on a charged oil droplet.
Setup
Oil sprayed between two parallel plates. Plates connected to variable HT supply. View droplets through microscope. Adjust voltage until a droplet is stationary.
Key Equations
Equilibrium: qE = mg
Electric field: E = V/d
Charge: q = mgd/V
Result: q = ne → e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C
Significance
First direct measurement of the elementary charge. Showed charge is quantised in units of e.
Drop levitated when electric force qE exactly balances gravity mg
10. Hooke's Law — Spring Extension Mechanics Basic
Aim
Verify that extension of a spring is proportional to the applied force (within the elastic limit).
Setup
Spring clamped vertically. Add 100 g masses one at a time. Measure extension x with a ruler after each addition. Record F = mg.
Key Equations
Hooke's Law: F = kx (within elastic limit)
Spring constant: k = F/x [N/m]
Graph: F vs x → straight line; gradient = k
Elastic PE: E = ½kx²
Graph Features
Linear region (Hooke's Law obeyed) → elastic limit → plastic region (permanent deformation) → breaking point.
F vs x graph: linear up to elastic limit (EL), then plastic deformation
